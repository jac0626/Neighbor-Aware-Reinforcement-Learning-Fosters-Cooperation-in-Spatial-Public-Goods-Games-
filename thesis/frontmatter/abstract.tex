\begin{abstract}
空间公共物品博弈中，合作者承担个体成本，而合作收益由周围成员共同分享。对于通过试错调整策略的智能体而言，仅依赖自身反馈未必能够维持群体合作；邻居的行为和收益可以提供额外参考，但这些信息一旦失真，也可能把价值学习引向错误方向。本文围绕这一矛盾研究局部社会信息如何进入个体强化学习，并进一步讨论在动作和奖励报告不可靠时，接收者应如何选择参考对象、调节其影响。

第一项工作研究可靠信息条件下的邻居经验利用。在标准 Q-learning 的时序差分更新之后，根据参考邻居相对自身的综合奖励优势以及双方动作是否一致，对个体已执行动作的 Q 值施加附加修正。已发表实验表明，适当的邻居影响可以改变合作演化轨迹，使部分条件下初期下降的合作在后期恢复；影响强度、奖励组成和感知范围共同决定这一过程，更早形成合作与更高的后期合作水平并不总是一致。

第二项工作进一步考虑邻居动作和奖励报告可能失真的情况。针对最高奖励规则中“参考谁”和“影响多大”两个脆弱环节，本文构造基于局部消息与自身经验的线性评分器和连续门控，分别控制参考对象和附加更新幅度。共享参数通过完整群体学习轨迹上的交叉熵搜索获得，测试时保持冻结，个体 Q 表仍在新环境中从头在线学习。对更新式的分析表明，参考消息的动作语义决定附加项对合作价值优势的作用方向，门控负责缩放这一方向；局部归一化还给出单步修正上界，并可进一步推出有界奖励下的 Q 值范围。

冻结参数后的最终留出测试覆盖$r=4.4$、4.8以及干净、10\%持续虚假高奖励背叛报告四个条件，每个方法—条件组合包含30次独立环境运行。完整模型在两个受扰条件下的全程合作率分别为0.96774和0.98398；相对已调强度的固定合作规则，四个条件中的全程合作提高4.105至5.255个百分点，相应四项Bonferroni校正区间均为正。与此同时，固定合作评分加学习门控已经解释了大部分提升：进一步学习评分只在部分条件提供增量，并在$r=4.8$干净条件下出现小幅性能代价。特征和幅度实验表明，当前控制器依赖显式合作语义，同时使用自身历史和价值状态；不同消息、协同因子、异常比例及感知范围下的结果也显示，更复杂的评分并非始终优于简化规则。因此，本文将该方法定位为面向合作任务的局部信息控制，而不是一般意义上的消息真假识别。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract*}
In spatial public goods games, cooperators bear individual costs while the resulting benefits are shared with nearby participants. For agents that adapt through trial and error, learning only from their own feedback does not necessarily sustain group cooperation. Neighbors' actions and rewards can provide useful reference information, but distorted reports may also steer value learning in the wrong direction. This thesis studies how local social information can be incorporated into individual reinforcement learning and, when reported actions and rewards are unreliable, how a receiver should choose whom to reference and how strongly that information should affect learning.

The first part studies the use of neighbor experience under reliable information. After the standard temporal-difference update of Q-learning, an additional correction is applied to the value of the action just taken, based on the reference neighbor's relative composite reward and whether the two agents' actions agree. Published experiments show that appropriate neighbor influence can reshape cooperation dynamics and, under some conditions, allow cooperation to recover after an initial decline. The outcome depends jointly on influence strength, reward composition, and perception range, and earlier recovery does not always coincide with a higher late-stage cooperation level.

The second part considers distorted action and reward reports. To address the two vulnerable steps in the highest-reward reference rule---whom to select and how strongly to follow the selected message---a linear scorer is built from local reports and the receiver's own experience, while a continuous gate controls the magnitude of the additional Q-value update. Shared controller parameters are optimized by cross-entropy search over complete population-learning trajectories. They are frozen during testing, whereas each agent's Q-table is learned online from scratch in every new environment. Analysis of the update rule shows that the reported action determines the direction of the correction on the cooperation-value gap, while the gate scales its magnitude. Local normalization also yields a bound on each additional update and, under bounded rewards, a conservative range for all Q-values.

The frozen models are evaluated under $r=4.4$ and 4.8 with clean communication or 10\% persistent false high-reward defection reports, using 30 independent environment runs for every method-condition pair. The full controller achieves whole-trajectory cooperation of 0.96774 and 0.98398 in the two distorted-message conditions. Across all four conditions, it improves whole-trajectory cooperation by 4.105--5.255 percentage points over a tuned fixed cooperative rule, and the corresponding four Bonferroni-adjusted intervals are positive. However, a learned gate with fixed cooperative scoring accounts for most of this improvement: learned scoring provides additional gains only in some conditions and incurs a small clean-performance cost at $r=4.8$. Feature and magnitude analyses show that the frozen controller relies strongly on explicit cooperative semantics while also using the receiver's own history and value state. Experiments over different message types, synergy factors, fault fractions, and perception ranges further show that the more flexible scorer is not uniformly superior to simpler rules. The resulting method is therefore best understood as task-oriented local information control for cooperation, rather than a general detector of truthful messages.
\end{abstract*}
